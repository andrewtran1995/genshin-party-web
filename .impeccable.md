## Design Context

### Users

Family and close friends — extended family across Korean and Vietnamese communities, close friends averaging early 30s. They visit the site a small number of times: once to get the details, once to RSVP. They're not power users; they're guests who care about feeling welcomed and in-the-know. Mobile is likely the dominant context (checking the link from a group chat).

### Brand Personality

**Playful, editorial, polished.**

The site should feel like a beautifully typeset invitation that someone made with genuine care and a wink. Not stiff wedding-industry elegance, not chaotic DIY. Something warm and a little surprising — where the typography does the heavy lifting and small moments of delight reward attention.

### Aesthetic Direction

- **Theme**: Light. This is a celebratory site viewed in everyday, bright contexts (commuting, at home, sharing with family).
- **Color**: The existing palette (gold, green, blue, lavender) is warm and intentional. Use it with confidence rather than timidity — accents should feel present, not apologetic. Use darker variants on white backgrounds where legibility demands it.
- **Typography**: Three custom display fonts (Dog Days, Mantra Modern, Sunliner Revival) are a strong foundation. Dog Days owns the "big moment" (event date display). Mantra Modern for headings. Sunliner Revival for body. Respect this hierarchy.
- **Motion**: Purposeful and restrained. One well-timed entrance or state change beats five micro-interactions. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Reference**: spencergabor.work — appreciated for its editorial typography control, staggered reveals, and spring-physics interactions. The playfulness comes from _how_ elements arrive, not from decorating everything.
- **Anti-reference**: Generic wedding site templates — hero image of rings, cursive everything, soft pink/blush palettes, stock photography aesthetic.

### Design Principles

1. **Typography is the decoration.** The custom fonts carry the visual personality. Don't paper over them with icons, badges, or graphic elements.
2. **Warm but not saccharine.** The palette is cheerful — use it to feel festive, not like a confetti explosion.
3. **Small surprises, quiet confidence.** The site should feel like it was made by someone who knows what they're doing and enjoyed it. Whimsy lives in the details.
4. **Guests first.** Every page is a task: find the info, RSVP, leave. Don't get in the way of that with decoration. Delight should enhance the task, not distract from it.
5. **Accessible and cross-cultural.** Three languages, multiple generations. Keep the interface clear and keyboard-navigable. Motion should never be required to understand the UI.
